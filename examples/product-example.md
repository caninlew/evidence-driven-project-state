# Product Example

## Scenario

A product team is preparing a pricing-page redesign and pricing-policy update.

Raw inputs include:

- product brief
- sales feedback
- finance comments
- support tickets
- analytics summary

## Why This Framework Helps

In a normal workflow, the team often mixes:

- customer complaints
- internal assumptions
- already-approved pricing rules
- unapproved growth ideas

This framework separates them before execution.

## Example State Breakdown

### Source observations

- Sales notes say enterprise prospects perceive the pricing page as unclear.
- Finance comments suggest annual billing discounts may need revision.
- Support tickets show repeated confusion about seat limits.

### Current facts

- The current public pricing page has three plans.
- Seat-limit explanation is currently split across pricing and FAQ pages.

### Stakeholder statements

- Growth lead wants a simplified two-tier structure.
- Finance team prefers keeping the existing plan count until margin review is complete.

### Open questions

- Has leadership approved a pricing-structure change, or only a page redesign?
- Which seat-limit policy is the current official baseline?
- Is annual discount policy already fixed for the next quarter?

### Bounded tasks

- Create a source register for all pricing-related inputs.
- Produce a current-state summary that distinguishes approved policy from proposed changes.
- Prepare a conflict register for pricing-structure options.

## Result

Instead of jumping straight into redesign, the team gets:

- a traceable baseline
- visible conflicts
- explicit unanswered questions
- cleaner handoff into product design and implementation
