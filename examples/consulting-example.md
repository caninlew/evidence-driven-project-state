# Consulting Example

## Scenario

A consulting team is running a strategy project for a regional retail client.

Raw inputs include:

- client brief
- workshop notes
- market scans
- interview summaries
- draft financial model comments

## Why This Framework Helps

Consulting projects often blur together:

- what the client requested
- what interviews suggested
- what analysts inferred
- what the team recommends

This framework keeps those layers separate.

## Example State Breakdown

### Source observations

- Workshop notes indicate the client wants to expand into two adjacent cities.
- Interview summaries suggest store managers are worried about staffing capacity.
- Draft model comments note that current revenue assumptions may be optimistic.

### Current facts

- The client currently operates in one city with 18 stores.
- The brief asks for a 3-year expansion strategy.

### Stakeholder statements

- Client leadership prefers a fast rollout if capital requirements remain moderate.
- Regional operations managers prefer phased expansion.

### Open questions

- Has the client formally approved expansion into both adjacent cities, or is that still exploratory?
- Which staffing assumptions should be used as the working baseline?
- Is the draft financial model approved for decision use?

### Bounded tasks

- Register all source materials with authority and status.
- Produce a current-state baseline before recommendation writing.
- Separate confirmed business facts from analyst recommendations.

## Result

The project team gets:

- a cleaner evidence trail
- less confusion between analysis and recommendation
- better reviewability for partners and client leads
- easier onboarding for new team members joining mid-project
