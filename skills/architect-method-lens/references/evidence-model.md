# Evidence Model

Use this model whenever collecting sources, assigning confidence, or auditing a claim.

## Source levels

| Level | Source type | Typical examples | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Architect-authored primary source | book, essay, lecture, interview, office statement | Attribute a stated position, with date and locator |
| P2 | Primary project evidence | drawings, specifications, model, construction record, verified building documentation | Support observable design operations |
| S1 | Scholarly or curated secondary source | peer-reviewed paper, academic book, museum archive, critical monograph | Contextualize, compare, or interpret |
| S2 | Professional editorial source | reputable journal, recorded review, project publication | Supplement facts and criticism |
| U | Unverified or weak source | unattributed post, image board, generated summary, marketing copy | Discovery only; do not support a substantive claim |

Being primary does not guarantee truth. An architect's retrospective narrative may simplify collaboration, chronology, or causality. Note these limitations.

## Claim labels

- **Documented**: Explicitly supported by P1, or by reliable project records for a factual claim.
- **Observed**: Directly visible or measurable in P2 material; avoid assigning intent.
- **Interpreted**: A reasoned reading supported by observations and preferably S1 criticism.
- **Provisional**: Plausible but based on an incomplete source set, too few projects, or unresolved contradictions.

## Confidence rubric

Assign confidence to a method card, not to the architect as a whole.

- **High**: Supported across at least three projects, includes primary evidence, and survives counterexample review.
- **Medium**: Supported across multiple sources or projects but depends on interpretation, a narrow period, or incomplete records.
- **Low**: Based on one or two projects, secondary summaries, or contested attribution. Keep as provisional.

Do not convert counts into false precision or percentages.

## Triangulation rules

1. Use at least one P1 or P2 source for a documented method claim.
2. Use at least three materially different projects for a recurring method.
3. Seek one limiting or contradictory case for every high-confidence card.
4. Record career period and project conditions; do not merge incompatible periods.
5. Distinguish an office method from an individual author's intent when collaborators matter.
6. Cite page, timestamp, drawing number, archive identifier, or stable URL when available.

## Visual evidence rules

Describe visible relations before interpretation: sequence, dimension, alignment, enclosure, opening, material joint, structure, light condition, or use pattern. State image limitations such as unknown lens, crop, staging, season, or absence of scale.

Never infer user experience, construction performance, climate performance, or social outcome from a single photograph.

## Conflict handling

When sources disagree:

1. State the conflict directly.
2. Compare date, proximity to the work, authorship, and evidence type.
3. Preserve multiple readings when the record cannot resolve them.
4. Lower confidence rather than selecting the most attractive interpretation.

## Minimum source register fields

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| ID | Stable short identifier |
| Creator | Architect, office, author, photographer, or archive |
| Title / project | Exact title when known |
| Date / period | Publication date and project date when relevant |
| Level | P1, P2, S1, S2, or U |
| Locator | Page, timestamp, drawing, figure, or URL |
| Supports | Claim or method-card IDs |
| Limitations | Missing context, attribution, access, translation, or visual limits |
